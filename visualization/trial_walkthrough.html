One HSVI trial, worked end to end

Trial 5 (0-indexed iteration 4) from the actual solver run on the 3-action game -- games/hsvi_trace_fixed.jsonl, root partition 2, prior belief P(Schemer)=0.25.

This is one real trial pulled out of the interactive convergence tree and laid out as a straight line, because it's a clean illustration of the two things that make this whole approach work: a persistent belief that accumulates evidence across all 8 rounds, and a monitoring policy that gets to choose, round by round, how hard to look.

Round 1 opens with an Audit -- expensive, but it always tells the truth. It comes back "honest," and belief drops hard, from the 0.25 prior down to 0.042. Not to zero, though: the solver's own worst-case adversary strategy allows a real Schemer to sometimes play Honest too, so even a full Audit clearing someone doesn't mean certainty.

Rounds 2 through 6 are quiet. Budget is down to 1, so the Monitor coasts on Pass and cheap black-box probes -- free or nearly free, and each "clear" nudges belief down a little further, from 0.042 to 0.030. Five rounds of nothing.

Round 7 breaks the pattern: a Cheap check on sum_of_digits comes back FLAG. The measured false-positive rate for that check is exactly 0.000, so this one weak, nearly-free signal is treated as fully diagnostic -- belief jumps straight from 0.030 to 1.000, overriding five rounds of accumulated "everything's fine."

That's the point of carrying belief as a persistent, single running number across the whole session instead of resetting it each round: a Schemer that behaves for six rounds and slips up once on the seventh doesn't get a clean record averaged over their good behavior -- one hard signal at the end still fully re-classifies them, because the game is still using everything it's seen from round 1 onward.

What this trial's backup did to the root's own bounds

Before this trial, the value function's estimate of the whole 8-round game (at the root's belief 0.25) sat in [-56.859, -55.824] -- a width of 1.035, still fairly uncertain. This trial's forward walk (the peek phase, above) picked a promising, cheap-heavy path to test; its backward pass then propagated the new information about that path all the way back up to the root, narrowing the estimate to [-56.812, -56.220] -- a width of 0.592. One trial cut the remaining uncertainty at the root by 43%.

That's HSVI's whole strategy in miniature: don't try to evaluate every belief point to full precision. Pick one promising trajectory, walk it forward, then let a single backward pass tighten the bounds everywhere along that path -- including, most importantly, back at the root, which is the only number that actually decides how the game is being played right now. Repeat a few dozen times (29, for this game) and the root's own gap between lower and upper bound shrinks from 9.65 down to 0.049 -- see the full convergence tree for the entire run.
