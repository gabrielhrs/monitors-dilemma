The attacker's worst-case strategy

Every other page here reconstructs the Monitor's policy. This one reconstructs the other side: at each belief P(Schemer), what does the solver's own worst-case adversary actually do, given that it secretly is the Schemer? This isn't free arithmetic the way the Monitor's policy was -- the follower's mixed strategy is a genuine LP artifact (a dual/primal solution), computed transiently inside selectAO() during exploration and never persisted the way a StrategyNode's .strategy is. Reconstructing it means re-solving a small LP at each swept belief point (Partition::getLowerboundLP + LPHandle::getPlayer2Strategy()), against the value function's already-fixed vector set -- read-only relative to the solved game, just not free the way the Monitor's sweep was. Only the Schemer row of the result means anything: the Benign state's nominal "Attack" action is a no-op indistinguishable from Honest, so its slice of the strategy carries no information and is omitted here.
P(Attack) -- worst-case Schemer's probability of attacking, at this belief nVectors ≤ 1: partition rarely/never actually backed up -- treat the curve as noise, same caveat as the Monitor pages
The headline finding: the worst-case Schemer attacks almost everywhere, almost always. Across every partition that's actually been trained (nVectors ≥ 2), P(Attack) sits mostly in the 0.8-1.0 range for any belief above 0 -- not the cagey, occasionally-honest adversary you might expect given the earlier finding that an Audit's "honest" result doesn't always collapse belief to exactly 0. Those two findings aren't in tension: even a small P(Honest) is enough to keep a posterior belief nonzero after a clean Audit (Bayes' rule doesn't need much), and the softening that does show up here is concentrated in a handful of partitions (2, 4, 5, 10, 11, 13, 14, 16, 17 -- mostly budget-1/2, mid-game rounds) rather than spread evenly. The guarantee the Monitor's policy achieves isn't built around facing a rarely-attacking adversary -- it's built to hold up against one that takes almost every opening it gets.

Round x audit-budget grid -- P(Attack) vs. belief P(Schemer)

Each cell: x-axis is belief P(Schemer), swept 0 to 1 -- not an iteration count, there's no solver progress on this page at all, every point is read off the same already-solved value function. y-axis is the worst-case Schemer's P(Attack) at that belief, 0 (always stays Honest) to 1 (always Attacks) -- the shaded/filled area is exactly that probability.
One outlier worth flagging, not over-reading: partition 20 (round 7, sum_of_digits, budget 2) is the only trained partition where P(Attack) touches exactly 0 -- at belief 0.25 specifically, the worst-case Schemer stays perfectly honest. It's a real LP solution, not a plotting artifact, but nVectors there is only 2, in the same lightly-trained territory as most of this page, so it's a repeatable finding rather than a settled one -- the kind of thing worth re-checking once/if this partition gets visited by more trials.
A mechanical edge case, not a finding: every partition shows a discontinuity right at belief=0 (P(Attack) drops to exactly 0, then jumps as soon as belief ticks up to 0.01). That's a boundary artifact of sweeping down to a belief where P(Schemer)=0 exactly -- the LP still returns some strategy for the hypothetical "you secretly are the Schemer" row even when the belief weighting on that row is zero, and there's nothing pinning that degenerate case to match the limit as belief→0. It's the same category of edge case as the untrained partitions -- worth excluding from the read, not a qualitative result.
