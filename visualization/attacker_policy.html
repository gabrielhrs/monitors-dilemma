The attacker's worst-case strategy

Every other page here reconstructs the Monitor's policy. This one reconstructs the other side: at each belief P(Schemer), what does the solver's own worst-case adversary actually do, given that it secretly is the Schemer? This isn't free arithmetic the way the Monitor's policy was -- the follower's mixed strategy is a genuine LP artifact (a dual/primal solution), computed transiently inside the solver during exploration and never persisted the way the Monitor's per-vector strategy is. Reconstructing it means re-solving a small LP at each swept belief point, against the value function's already-fixed vector set -- read-only relative to the solved game, just not free the way the Monitor's sweep was. Only the Schemer row of the result means anything: the Benign state's nominal "Attack" action is a no-op indistinguishable from Honest, so its slice of the strategy carries no information and is omitted here.
P(Attack) -- worst-case Schemer's probability of attacking, at this belief nVectors ≤ 1: partition rarely/never actually backed up -- treat the curve as noise, same caveat as the Monitor pages
The headline finding: the worst-case Schemer attacks almost everywhere, almost always. Across every partition that's actually been trained (nVectors ≥ 2), P(Attack) sits mostly in the 0.8-1.0 range for any belief above 0 -- not the cagey, occasionally-honest adversary you might expect given the earlier finding that an Audit's "honest" result doesn't always collapse belief to exactly 0. Those two findings aren't in tension: even a small P(Honest) is enough to keep a posterior belief nonzero after a clean Audit (Bayes' rule doesn't need much), and the softening that does show up here is concentrated in just four partitions (2, 4, 17, 20 -- see below for 20 specifically) rather than spread evenly. The guarantee the Monitor's policy achieves isn't built around facing a rarely-attacking adversary -- it's built to hold up against one that takes almost every opening it gets.

Round x audit-budget grid -- P(Attack) vs. belief P(Schemer)

Each cell: x-axis is belief P(Schemer), swept 0 to 1 -- not an iteration count, there's no solver progress on this page at all, every point is read off the same already-solved value function. y-axis is the worst-case Schemer's P(Attack) at that belief, 0 (always stays Honest) to 1 (always Attacks) -- the shaded/filled area is exactly that probability.
One outlier worth flagging: partition 20 (round 7, sum_of_digits, budget 2) is the only trained partition where P(Attack) touches exactly 0 -- and not just at a single point, but across the whole range from belief 0.05 to 1.0: the worst-case Schemer stays perfectly honest here regardless of how certain the Monitor already is. It's a real LP solution, not a plotting artifact, and it has a clean explanation once you cross-reference the reconstructed Monitor policy: partition 20 is the one place the Monitor still has its full 2-audit budget this late (round 7 with nothing spent in rounds 1-6), and its policy there is deterministic Audit for any belief above 0.02 -- a round with certain detection if you attack. The worst-case Schemer's best response to facing a near-certain Audit is simply not to attack yet, and wait for a round where the Monitor's budget is already spent and only Cheap or Pass remain. nVectors here is only 2, in the same lightly-trained territory as most of this page, so it's a repeatable finding rather than a fully settled one -- but the mechanism behind it checks out against the Monitor's own reconstructed policy, not just a bare LP output.
A mechanical edge case, not a finding: every partition shows a discontinuity right at belief=0 (P(Attack) drops to exactly 0, then jumps as soon as belief ticks up to 0.01). That's a boundary artifact of sweeping down to a belief where P(Schemer)=0 exactly -- the LP still returns some strategy for the hypothetical "you secretly are the Schemer" row even when the belief weighting on that row is zero, and there's nothing pinning that degenerate case to match the limit as belief→0. It's the same category of edge case as the untrained partitions -- worth excluding from the read, not a qualitative result.
