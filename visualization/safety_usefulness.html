Safety vs. usefulness: what the blended reward was actually trading off

Every benchmark shown so far -- no audits, the best/worst fixed 2-audit schedules, and the solved adaptive policy -- gets compared on one blended number: cost(a1) + P(real attack) * (1 - catch_prob) * C_CATASTROPHE, summed and discounted over 8 rounds. That number is genuinely what the solver optimizes, but it hides the trade-off inside a single scalar. This page splits it back into the two things actually being traded: how much monitoring overhead was spent (usefulness cost), and how much expected real-attack probability mass slipped through uncaught (safety, lower is better). Neither quantity was tracked separately anywhere in the original pipeline -- both are reconstructed here, the fixed schedules from a closed-form split of naive_baseline.py's existing formula, and the solved adaptive point by walking the actual game tree under the reconstructed Monitor policy and attacker policy sweeps, replicating the solver's own belief-update math by hand.

Four policies, two axes

x-axis: expected discounted usefulness cost (monitoring overhead spent, always ≤ 0 -- closer to 0, further right, is better/cheaper). y-axis: expected discounted missed-attack probability mass (lower is safer) -- plotted with low values toward the top, so up-and-right is the direction both axes agree is better, same as the x-axis.

The headline finding: the solved adaptive policy has the lowest missed-attack mass of all four points -- safer than even the best fixed 2-audit schedule -- while spending less on monitoring overhead than either fixed-audit schedule (worst or best). It isn't a compromise between the fixed schedules' safety and no-audits' usefulness; it Pareto-dominates both fixed-audit schedules outright, on both axes at once. Only "no audits" beats it on usefulness, and only by doing effectively nothing -- its missed-attack mass is by far the worst of the four, roughly 46% higher than the solved policy's.
On the reconstruction gap: the solved-adaptive point's blended value () sits close to, but outside, the actual converged root bound from games/hsvi_trace_fixed.jsonl. That's expected, not a bug: this point comes from re-walking the game tree with nearest-neighbor lookups into two independent 0.01-belief-resolution sweeps at each of 8 rounds (compounding grid-snap error), rather than reading the solver's own alpha-vectors directly. The underlying single-step belief-update math was checked directly against the real solver trace and matches closely: reconstructing the same step shown in the worked trial example (an Audit at the root coming back "honest") gives belief 0.0435 here vs. 0.042 logged by the actual solver.
