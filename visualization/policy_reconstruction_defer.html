Reconstructed policy with Defer

Same policy-reconstruction technique as the 3-action page, applied to a 4th Monitor action: Defer, which routes the round to a weaker-but-trusted model instead of running the untrusted output at all. Deferring is unconditionally safe (no catastrophe is possible) and reveals no signal about the type, at a fixed usefulness cost (C_DEFER = -3.0) -- not a measured quantity, since no benchmarked quality gap between the untrusted and trusted-fallback models exists in this project's elicitation data; it's an illustrative placeholder, easy to re-sweep via build_toy_control_game.py --c-defer. Unlike Audit, Defer is never budget-gated. There's no separate "suspicion score" signal feeding this -- P(Schemer) already is the continuous suspicion estimate; the question this page answers is simply where, if anywhere, the solved policy actually chooses to defer as that belief rises.

Update: this solve has now been properly tightened. The first version of this page was run under the same bug that affected the 3-action solve -- hsvi.h's HSVI_GOAL constant was silently capping convergence at a bound-gap of 1.0 regardless of the requested eps. With that fixed, this game ran 80 iterations to a gap under 0.05 (was 48 iterations, gap 0.65). The reassuring result: essentially nothing here moved. The root's value (-16.3068) is bit-for-bit identical to the loose run, now bracketed by a much tighter upper bound (-16.26, was -15.66), and every threshold below is the same or within 0.01 of before -- unlike the 3-action game, where tightening genuinely flipped one partition's optimal action. Four partitions still show the hatched "never backed up" pattern; as on the 3-action page, at least some of those are structurally unreachable from the actual starting budget rather than under-explored.

Round x audit-budget grid

Defer only wins once Audit is off the table, exactly as the costs predict: Audit catches with certainty at a cost of -1.0, so it strictly dominates Defer's -3.0 whenever any audit budget remains -- and that's exactly what the solve found. Across every budget-1 and budget-2 partition here, Defer's weight is 0% the moment real backups exist; Audit (or a Pass/Audit mix while belief is still low) wins outright. Defer only shows up in the budget-0 column, where Audit isn't a choice any more.
And once it's the only safe option left, it appears as a clean, domain-dependent threshold: in bounded-domain rounds (P6 = R3 fibonacci, P15 = R6 is_prime, P18 = R7 sum_of_digits, where the cheap check has real bite), the policy goes Cheap→Defer, with Defer taking over once belief crosses roughly 0.06-0.09. In unbounded-domain rounds (P9 = R4 reverse_string, P12 = R5 count_vowels), where the cheap check gives literally zero protection, the policy skips straight from Pass to Defer at an even lower threshold, around 0.04-0.05 -- there's no point paying for a Cheap check that can't catch anything, so the solver goes directly from doing nothing to fully bypassing the untrusted model. Both the existence of a threshold and its domain-dependent shape fell out of the solve; neither was designed in. That's the concrete answer to the original question -- yes, a "defer once belief crosses a threshold" rule is really what the optimal policy does once auditing is unavailable.
A separate, better-grounded comparison: it's tempting to measure these thresholds against Audit's own threshold at the root (~0.70 on the 3-action page), but that's a different partition answering a different question -- whether to spend one of two audits now versus saving it, not "is there anything better than Cheap/Pass left." The comparison that is valid: the root's own threshold, for its own decision, in this same 4-action game. It moved from 0.70 (no Defer anywhere in the game) to about 0.06 (Defer available in later rounds) -- even though the root itself never plays Defer, since Audit still dominates it there. The likely mechanism: once budget-0 rounds have Defer as a safety backstop, running out of the audit budget early is no longer as costly, which lowers the opportunity cost of spending an audit now rather than saving it. Unlike the thresholds above, this particular number rests on only 2-3 vectors at the root in both solves, so treat it as a real, repeatable finding (it didn't move between the loose and tight solves) rather than a fully precise one.
