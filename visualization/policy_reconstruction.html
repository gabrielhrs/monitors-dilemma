Reconstructed belief-to-action policy

Every trial we've shown so far played out one belief path at a time. This page asks a different question: at any belief P(Schemer), which action does the solved game actually recommend? For each partition (a round x audit-budget pair), the solver keeps a set of lower-bound alpha-vectors, each carrying a StrategyNode with a mixed action attached. getValue(belief) = max_j alpha_j · belief already picks the best vector -- the only change needed (getBestVector, added to AlphaVectorValueFunction) was to expose which vector j won, so we can read off its attached strategy. That's the same move as the alpha-vector-to-policy reconstruction from the L4DC paper, applied here to sweep belief from 0 to 1 and see where the recommended action actually changes -- no LP solving involved, just evaluating the alpha-vectors already sitting in memory after solve() returns.
Pass Cheap Audit never backed up (still the arbitrary uniform default)
Caveat first, updated: an earlier version of this page ran only 6 HSVI iterations and quoted a 0.46 threshold at the root -- that number turned out to be real but badly imprecise. A bug was found and fixed (hsvi.h's HSVI_GOAL constant was silently capping every solve at a bound-gap of 1.0, regardless of the eps passed on the command line), and this page now reflects a genuinely tightened solve: 29 iterations, gap under 0.05. 8 of the 24 partitions were untrained at 6 iterations; re-solving with the fix trained 4 of those, leaving 4 still hatched below. Two of those (round 1 at budget 0, round 2 at budget 0) are structurally unreachable from the actual starting budget of 2, so there's nothing to train -- HSVI is a targeted search, not exhaustive dynamic programming, and correctly has no reason to visit belief points that can never occur. The other two are more likely genuinely under-explored still.

Round x audit-budget grid

An emergent threshold, not a designed one -- now more precisely located: partition 2, round 1 at full budget, is the root of the whole game. Its policy is a mostly-Audit mix for belief up to about 0.70, then flips to deterministic Audit above that -- a real threshold, just further out than the earlier imprecise solve suggested. Partition 4 (round 2, budget 1, reached right after an early audit) shows the same shape with its own threshold around 0.24; partition 10 (round 4, budget 1) shows it again around 0.29, essentially unchanged from before. The budget-0 partitions (6, 15, 18 -- no audits left to spend) show a much lower Pass→Cheap threshold, now consistently around belief 0.03. None of these thresholds were designed in -- they fell out of solving the game, and held up (with corrected locations) under tighter convergence.
What tighter convergence actually caught: partition 16 (round 6, budget 1) didn't just move its threshold -- it flipped which action wins outright. The imprecise solve had it settling on deterministic Audit above belief 0.14; the tightened solve instead settles on deterministic Cheap above belief 0.07, with Audit's weight dropping to zero. That's a plausible, rational reversal rather than noise: round 6 is bounded-domain, where Cheap gives real (18%) protection, but round 8 two rounds later is unbounded, where Cheap gives none at all and Audit is the only thing that works. With only one audit left at this point, it's better to spend it protecting the round where nothing else can help, and accept Cheap's partial protection here where it can. The looser solve hadn't yet worked that trade-off out; the tighter one has. It's a reminder that "an emergent threshold" and "the right threshold" aren't the same claim -- the first one was visible even at 6 iterations, but trusting the second needed the fix.
